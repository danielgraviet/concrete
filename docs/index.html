01 / The workspace

Everything starts with a note.

Concrete keeps your source material close while you work with it. Write in Markdown, ask questions grounded in your notes, and turn what you know into practice.

01 — Keep the source

Notes stay yours.

Plain Markdown files live in your vault on your Mac. Organize them into folders, link ideas, and open them in any editor.

02 — Make connections

Practice what you learn.

Build quizzes from selected notes and review flashcards with spaced repetition.

03 — Keep moving

Ask better questions.

Work with an AI tutor that can use your material as context. Choose and configure the provider that fits your workflow.

02 / The mark

One idea.
Many forms.

Concrete’s identity starts with a square: divide it into halves, split it into quarters, then repeat the module to make a larger system. The same spirit runs through the workspace—small notes, linked into something useful.

Build a little.

Opening Concrete for the first time
  1. Open the downloaded DMG and drag Concrete into Applications.
  2. If macOS blocks this unsigned build, open Terminal and run xattr -cr /Applications/Concrete.app.
  3. Open Concrete, or right-click it and choose Open. If needed, allow it under System Settings → Privacy & Security.
